# Slide Art-Direction Knowledge

Use this knowledge to synthesize original visual direction for fixed-canvas video
Slides. It is distilled from measured layouts, semantic motion-graphic scenes,
and reviewed visual systems. It is design vocabulary, not a catalog of finished
styles.

## Use Of The Knowledge

- Start from the confirmed video purpose, audience, tone, evidence character, and
  Slide intents.
- Select only compatible traits that clarify that content.
- Create a coherent visual thesis instead of naming or copying a reference style.
- Let the whole-video direction govern color behavior, typography character,
  material, geometry, density, and motion character.
- Let each supplied Slide relationship govern its composition.

Never map an industry or topic directly to a preset. Never force a source trait
onto content merely because the source example looked successful.

## Palette Vocabulary

Available evidence-supported behaviors include:

- warm paper or linen fields with charcoal ink and scarce highlighter accents;
- pale technical fields with graphite ink and restrained cool accents;
- light editorial neutrals with muted earth or botanical accents;
- dark opaque fields with ivory text and one metallic or electric signal accent;
- full-bleed warm/cool color fields with white text and minimal annotation;
- narrow duotone print relationships;
- broad luminous palettes only when an immersive field is meaningful.

Define role relationships rather than decorative color lists. Preserve one clear
dominant field, readable ink, a primary signal, and a scarce highlight. Additional
colors must encode supplied distinctions. Do not make every Slide dark, bright,
gradient-heavy, or monochromatic by habit.

## Typography Vocabulary

Available evidence-supported behaviors include:

- monumental condensed display type with compact sans support;
- refined editorial serif display with neutral sans body text;
- typewriter or archival typography for source and evidence character;
- handwritten emphasis paired with clean instructional body text;
- monospace numerals and labels paired with readable technical body text;
- widely tracked labels used sparingly for navigation through a composition;
- strong display-to-body contrast for a single assertion;
- restrained hierarchy for explanation-dense material.

Use type to reveal hierarchy and voice. Limit the number of simultaneous roles.
Do not imitate brand typography, use remote fonts, or turn every fact into a large
headline.

## Material Vocabulary

Available evidence-supported behaviors include:

- flat editorial or poster fields;
- paper, grid-paper, archive-border, highlighter, and print-texture cues;
- fine rules, metric rails, evidence lists, and restrained framed regions;
- soft glow or liquid fields with limited depth;
- hard-edged signal blocks and technical grid cells;
- watercolor or organic washes used as spatial support;
- ornamental frames and plaques used only when formality supports the content.

Choose one dominant material logic for the video. Material must support reading,
not produce a decorative UI surface. Avoid generic card grids, excessive glass,
unmotivated shadows, and nested panels.

## Geometry Vocabulary

Available evidence-supported behaviors include:

- thin process rails, directional lines, and restrained connectors;
- horizontal metrics, segmented rings, bars, and direct annotations;
- hard signal blocks, geometric planes, and offset circles;
- hand-drawn marks, checks, stars, and rough connectors for teaching emphasis;
- frames, rules, diamonds, and corner structures for formal organization;
- progress rings, phase rails, node networks, and technical swatches;
- large primary forms with a small number of supporting objects;
- sparse aligned type with minimal framing.

Geometry must encode a supplied role or relationship. A connector requires an
upstream relationship; a sequence rail requires an upstream order; repeated forms
require upstream repeated objects. Never invent structure to fill empty space.

## Structural Range

The reviewed material supports four broad structural envelopes. Use them as
continuous design possibilities, not selectable presets:

- high occupancy with one large primary object and moderate supporting structure;
- dense structured composition with more geometry, connectors, and depth;
- type-led composition with a small primary object and strong typographic scale;
- sparse composition with a narrow palette and flat minimal framing.

Choose density from the supplied amount and complexity of information. Preserve a
single dominant anchor and a readable final frame in every envelope.

## Motion Vocabulary

Available evidence-supported behaviors include:

- measured layered reveal and masked title construction;
- sequential evidence, marker, node, or phase reveal;
- fine rule, connector, bar, ring, or check drawing;
- short directional wipes or block assembly;
- soft settle, bloom, wash dissolve, or blur-to-clear emphasis;
- limited spring or elastic accent for instructional or expressive material.

Motion direction describes character and visual consequence only. Exact timing
comes from the supplied `semantic_timeline`. Never reorder cues, invent a reveal
target, loop motion, or require interaction to understand the final meaning.

## Synthesis Standard

A useful direction names concrete visual behavior without prescribing code. It
should let the next designer answer, at a glance:

- What is the dominant visual anchor?
- How are the supplied objects spatially related?
- What visual hierarchy makes the supplied priority obvious?
- What graphic language supports this video's voice?
- How does the supplied presentation order become visible?

Reject vague directions such as "make it premium," "use an Apple style," or
"create a modern UI." Reject over-specified directions that repeat every token,
constraint, and timing value. Produce a short, content-native visual instruction.
